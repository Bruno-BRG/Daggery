# Duality GME

Rules version 0.1. An independent solo-play supplement for Daggerheart: take
**oaths**, make **moves**, track **progress**, and **Pay the Price** when things
go wrong. Daggerheart's action roll is the move roll. Use these rules alongside
the Daggerheart core rules, with paper and dice or whatever play aids you prefer.
No app or online service is required.

The complete rules reference is the **Duality GME Manual**
(`duality-solo-engine/duality-gme-manual.pdf`). The shorter
`duality-solo-engine/duality-gme.pdf` is a quick reference to the same system,
not a separate engine. This summary and the app follow the full manual.

## How to play

1. **Create your character** with the Daggerheart rules.
2. **Take an Oath** on an inciting incident (roll a Plot Hook if you need
   one). This is your first quest.
3. **Begin a Session**, every time you sit down to play.
4. **Envision, then make moves.** When you do something a move describes, make
   that move: usually an action roll with the trait that fits.
5. **Read the outcome** as a strong hit, weak hit or miss. On a miss, **Pay the
   Price**.
6. **Mark progress** on your oaths, expeditions, connections and scene
   challenges; roll **progress** to finish them.
7. When you stop, **End a Session**.

## Action roll outcomes

Make the Daggerheart action roll (Duality Dice + trait against a Difficulty),
then read it as a move outcome:

| Daggerheart result | Move outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Critical success | **Strong hit**, with the critical's own benefits |
| Success with Hope | **Strong hit** |
| Success with Fear | **Weak hit**: you get it, at a cost or with less |
| Failure with Hope | **Miss**: Pay the Price |
| Failure with Fear | **Miss**: Pay the Price; the GM may spend 1 Fear to make it severe |

Rolls with Fear still give the GM Fear, and rolls with Hope still give you Hope,
as the rules say. Hope is your momentum: spend it as Daggerheart allows.

**Difficulty by rank:** Minor 10, Serious 13, Grave 15, Dire
18, Legendary 20.

## Set the Difficulty

When an action calls for a roll, set the Difficulty **before rolling**. Use facts
already established in the scene, not the number you hope to beat.

### 1. Use an existing value first

1. If the rule or move specifies a Difficulty, use it.
2. If you are overcoming an adversary or NPC with a defined Difficulty, use theirs.
3. If the move tells you to use a challenge's rank, use that rank's Difficulty.

Do not add obstacle increases to these values. Only use the procedure below when
none of these sources provides the Difficulty. A nearby progress track does not
automatically set every action's Difficulty; the move must call for its rank.

### 2. Otherwise, start at 13

**13 is the default for an uncertain action.** You do not need to justify why it
is not harder. Check for three distinct obstacles:

| Obstacle | What must already be true? |
| --- | --- |
| **Resistance** | A relevant safeguard or committed opposition stands in your way: a reinforced lock, guards on alert, or someone under explicit orders to refuse. |
| **Demand** | The result requires unusual precision, reach or personal concession: a tiny mechanism, an exceptional gap, or a request that risks someone's position. |
| **Conditions** | Something concretely hinders the attempt: darkness without a light, unstable footing, or a crowd preventing conversation. |

| Distinct obstacles | Difficulty |
| --- | --- |
| None | **13** |
| One | **15** |
| Two | **18** |
| Three | **20** |

Count each category at most once. Count the same fact only once, even if it could
fit two categories. Ordinary uncertainty is already part of 13: a normal lock
is not automatically a Resistance obstacle. Do not invent obstacles to reach a
preferred number. If no established fact supports an obstacle, do not count it.

### 3. Easy, certain or impossible?

- **Difficulty 10:** no obstacles, and a concrete fact makes the task especially
  simple, but uncertainty remains.
- **No roll:** success is evident, or failure would change nothing.
- **Impossible right now:** do not roll; first change the situation.

Judge the task, not the hero. Traits and Experiences already modify the roll;
do not count them again when setting Difficulty. Do not also use the same fact
as an obstacle increase and as disadvantage, or as both the reason for Difficulty
10 and an advantage die. Separate facts may affect the roll separately.

### Example: convincing a guard

If the guard has a defined Difficulty, use it. Otherwise:

- Asking a willing person for ordinary information may need **no roll**.
- Convincing an undecided person to help with a reasonable task: **13**.
- Convincing a guard explicitly ordered to deny entry: Resistance, **15**.
- Asking that guard to abandon their post and risk their job: Resistance +
  Demand, **18**.
- Making that request amid a crowd that prevents conversation: all three, **20**.

### Lock it before rolling

Say the number and the reason: **"Difficulty 15: the guard has orders to refuse."**
Do not change it after seeing the dice. Reassess only when the situation changes.

**Danger is not Difficulty.** A simple crossing can still have a deadly fall.
Danger changes the consequence of failure; it does not automatically raise the
number. This is a Duality GME procedure for consistent rulings, not a guarantee
that every action has the same chance of success.

## Progress tracks

Oaths, expeditions, connections and scene challenges each have a **progress
track** of 10 boxes, and each box holds 4 ticks. The rank sets how much one
"mark progress" fills:

| Rank | Mark progress | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | 3 boxes | 10 |
| Serious | 2 boxes | 13 |
| Grave | 1 box | 15 |
| Dire | 2 ticks | 18 |
| Legendary | 1 tick | 20 |

**Progress roll:** when a move tells you to, roll two d10 (the challenge dice)
and compare each to your **progress score** (full boxes, 0 to 10). Beat both: a
strong hit. Beat one: a weak hit. Beat neither: a miss. Ties go to the challenge
dice. If the challenge dice match, something unexpected happens: a twist or an
extreme result. Progress rolls never generate Hope or Fear.

## Moves

Each move names its trigger and what each outcome does. The move groups are:

- **Session moves:** Begin a Session, Set a Flag, Change Your Fate, Take a Break,
  End a Session.
- **Quest moves:** Take an Oath, Reach a Milestone, Keep Your Oath,
  Break Your Oath.
- **Adventure moves:** Face Danger, Secure an Advantage, Gather Information,
  Compel, Make Camp.
- **Exploration moves:** Undertake an Expedition, Explore a Waypoint, Make a
  Discovery, Confront Chaos, Finish an Expedition.
- **Connection moves:** Make a Connection, Develop Your Relationship, Forge a
  Bond.
- **Scene challenge:** Begin the Scene, Face Danger or Secure an Advantage
  within it, Finish the Scene.
- **Combat moves:** Join the Fray, Battle, and the GM turn.
- **Fate moves:** Ask the Oracle, Pay the Price.

## Begin a Session

1. Set or review your flags (content you want to avoid or handle with care).
2. Recap where you left off.
3. Set the scene: where your character is and what they intend.
4. **Optional:** spotlight a danger, opportunity or insight away from your
   character: roll a **Vignette**, envision it, then gain a Hope as you return
   to your character.

## Ask the Oracle

Pick a likelihood and roll the Duality Dice; a yes needs the total shown. If the
dice match (doubles), the answer comes with an extreme result or a twist.

| Likelihood | Yes on | Chance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Almost certain | 7+ | 90% |
| Likely | 10+ | 75% |
| 50 / 50 | 13+ | 54% |
| Unlikely | 16+ | 31% |
| Small chance | 19+ | 15% |

For open questions, roll **Meaning** (Action + Subject, or Descriptor +
Subject).

## Pay the Price

On a miss, choose the most obvious negative outcome, or roll on the **Pay the
Price** table. A 12 means two prices. A **severe** price (failure with Fear, the
GM spends 1 Fear) rolls twice and both happen.

## Scene challenge

For a tense scene against a threat or a clock (a chase, an infiltration, a
debate): name the objective and give it a **grave** progress track, then a
**tension clock** of 4 segments if you are at a disadvantage, 6 on even footing,
8 with an advantage.

- **Face Danger or Secure an Advantage within the scene:** strong hit, mark
  progress; weak hit, mark progress and fill 1 segment; miss, fill 1 segment
  and Pay the Price. Failure with Fear fills 2 segments.
- **Finish the Scene** when the track is full, the clock is full, or the story
  ends it: a progress roll. Strong hit: you achieve the objective. Weak hit: you
  achieve it, at a cost or only in part. Miss: you fail, and Pay the Price.

## Exploration

**Undertake an Expedition** across dangerous ground: give it a rank and a
progress track. For each segment, roll Instinct (or Agility, or Knowledge as
fits) against the rank's Difficulty. Strong hit: mark progress, reach a
waypoint. Weak hit: mark progress and suffer a cost (a supply, a Stress, a
delay) or Pay the Price. Miss: Pay the Price, no progress.

At a waypoint, **Explore a Waypoint** with Instinct or Knowledge: a strong hit
finds an opportunity (**Find an Opportunity**) or, on a critical, a **Discovery**;
a weak hit gives a detail and a choice; a miss is a peril: Pay the Price. On a
failure with Fear, make a Fear Check: a pass spends 1 Fear and you **Confront
Chaos**. **Finish an Expedition** with a progress roll.

## Connections

**Make a Connection** (Presence): strong hit, they become a connection with a
role and a rank; weak hit, they ask something first; miss, Pay the Price.
**Develop Your Relationship** when you share a meaningful moment: mark progress.
**Forge a Bond** with a progress roll: strong hit, the bond is forged; once per
session they can help you (an advantage die, d6). Weak hit: they ask one more
thing first. Miss: something comes between you.

## Combat

**Join the Fray** with Instinct (or Agility): on a success with Hope you act
first; with Fear, or a failure, the GM takes the spotlight first. Fights then
use Daggerheart's stat blocks and the Duality combat tables for the GM turn
(Fear Check, Tactics, Fear Spend); a Hard Move result there is a severe Pay the
Price. For a minor fight, **Battle** instead: one
action roll. Strong hit: you win. Weak hit: you win, and Pay the Price. Miss:
you lose, and Pay the Price.

## Rest

Make Camp: take a Daggerheart rest, then roll Rest and Camp. A Random Event
result means: roll a Vignette.

## Quests and growth

Fulfilling an oath of Serious rank or higher is a milestone: level up when the
Daggerheart rules call for it. Forsaking an oath marks 2 Stress.
