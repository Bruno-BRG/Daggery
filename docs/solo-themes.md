# Daggery Solo: thematic scene pools

Version 0.1 — an original homebrew module for [Daggery Solo](solo-rules.md).
Works with lone hero, companion, and small-party play.

The goal is to make discoveries feel like parts of this particular adventure.
Choose a theme that fits the scene, connect it to something already in the
campaign, and interpret the dice as one concrete development.

## The three parts of a scene prompt

| Part | Answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Campaign anchor | Who or what does this concern? | The missing lighthouse keeper |
| Theme pack | What kind of material belongs here? | Mystery |
| Meaning pair | What detail or development appears? | A conflicting witness / points toward a trusted ally |

The scene check or action outcome supplies opportunity, trouble, or surprise.
The content rolls supply the subject matter. A scene prompt does not change a
yes/no answer, reverse an action result, apply damage, or create another GM turn.

## 1. Build a campaign pool

Keep a numbered list of **12 slots** containing live threads, NPCs, places,
factions, or unresolved clues. Use actual names and concerns rather than abstract
words. Each slot has an equal 1-in-12 chance when you roll for an anchor.

An entry may occupy two slots to make it recur more often: 2-in-12 instead of
1-in-12. For this module, use at most two slots per entry. An empty slot means
**choose the most relevant existing entry**, not automatically create another NPC.
Reserve one optional slot for **introduce something connected**.

You do not need separate long lists before starting. A useful initial pool is two
threads in four slots, two NPCs in four slots, one location, one faction, one clue,
and one opening for something new. Expand the variety as the adventure develops.

### Example: the Ashen Coast

| d12 | Campaign anchor |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Thread: discover why the lighthouse is dark |
| 2 | Thread: discover why the lighthouse is dark |
| 3 | Thread: bring the missing crew home |
| 4 | Thread: bring the missing crew home |
| 5 | NPC: Mara, the missing keeper |
| 6 | NPC: Mara, the missing keeper |
| 7 | NPC: Iven, the salvage captain |
| 8 | NPC: Iven, the salvage captain |
| 9 | Place: the flooded service tunnels |
| 10 | Faction: the Lantern Guild |
| 11 | Clue: a ledger of altered shipping routes |
| 12 | Introduce something connected to the current thread |

**When to draw an anchor:** when a twist, discovery, off-screen development, or
unclear world response needs a focus. If you already know who or what the event
concerns, use that anchor directly; do not randomize it again.

Roll a fresh d12 to select an anchor. Do not reuse the high or low die from the
scene check: doing so would make some numbered anchors systematically appear
more often in particular kinds of scenes.

At a scene's end, add a genuinely important discovery, retire a resolved entry,
or give a recurring element its second slot. Retired threads stay resolved; they
are not secretly reopened whenever you need a twist. A callback can show their
aftermath instead. Update the pool from what happened, not from whether you liked
the most recent draw. The pool is a list of story material, not a resource to spend.

## 2. Choose a theme pack

Choose based on the current question and setting, rather than randomly changing
genre every scene. A haunted court can use Court & Factions for negotiations and
Dread & Corruption for its supernatural disturbances.

| Pack | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Mystery | Investigations, evidence, missing people, uncertain explanations |
| Court & Factions | Negotiations, rival groups, patronage, political pressure |
| Ruins & Exploration | Forgotten structures, mechanisms, artifacts, discovery |
| Wilderness & Travel | Routes, weather, encounters, terrain, navigation |
| Dread & Corruption | Hauntings, unnatural influence, foreboding, temptation |
| Kinship & Community | Trust, belonging, recovery, obligations, local life |

Record one primary campaign theme and, optionally, a secondary theme. For a
blended scene, read the **subject from the primary pack** and the **development
from the secondary pack**. For example, a ruin's ceremonial chamber might be
involved in a faction's attempt to shift blame. This needs no extra blend roll.

### Read a theme pack

1. Name the scene question or the unknown detail you are generating.
2. Use a known anchor, or draw one if you need a focus.
3. Choose the appropriate theme pack or deliberate blend.
4. Roll fresh Hope and Fear d12s. Hope selects the **subject** column; Fear selects
   the **development** column. Do not add the dice together.
5. Combine the anchor and meaning pair into one event or detail, using the tone
   already established by the scene check, action result, or question oracle.
6. Record the interpretation and return to your character's next decision.

Each pack has **144 equally likely subject/development combinations**. The names
of the dice identify their columns; this content roll does not compare their
values to determine tone. Doubles are an ordinary content pair, not another
critical, twist, or invitation to roll recursively. No resources or clocks change.

If you are creating a detail with no prior tone, use the pair as written. Do not
force every detail to help or harm the hero. A useful question is “What can my
character notice, learn, or respond to here?”

## 4. Turn the same prompt toward Hope or Fear

The prompt's content and the scene's tone do different jobs. For example,
**a damaged record / connects two separate incidents**, linked to the Lantern Guild:

| Established tone | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Opportunity / Hope | A surviving page connects the disappearances to a shipment you can intercept. |
| Obstacle / Fear | The surviving page connects the disappearances, but the Guild is sealing the archive that holds the rest. |
| Twist / revelation | The same signature appears on both incidents; the signatory had a different role in each. Evidence can establish what that means. |
| No prior tone | A ledger page shows the same vessel present at both incidents. That is a lead, not proof of guilt. |

Neither Hope nor Fear dictates whether an NPC is morally good, whether a clue is
true, or whether the hero succeeds. Use the question oracle for a genuinely
uncertain proposition; use a character action for a risky attempt to act on it.

When an oracle already established a fact, keep it. “No, the keeper is not here”
cannot become “the keeper is in the next room” because of a theme prompt. A
conflicting witness might instead be a note about where the keeper went.

## 5. Scene purpose, when you need a starting point

If you have a location and theme but no useful scene question, choose or roll d12:

| d12 | Scene question |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | What can be learned before the evidence changes? |
| 2 | What agreement could let both sides move forward? |
| 3 | What is blocking passage, and what would open it? |
| 4 | What must be recovered before it leaves this place? |
| 5 | Who needs protection, and from what immediate risk? |
| 6 | What is keeping this system, custom, or mechanism from working? |
| 7 | What trail can still be followed? |
| 8 | What would make an escape or retreat possible? |
| 9 | What must be endured until help or an opening arrives? |
| 10 | What is happening here that changes an existing belief? |
| 11 | What important things cannot all be preserved? |
| 12 | What confrontation can no longer be postponed? |

Use the question to frame a choice, not to prescribe the hero's response. You
can reject an irrelevant purpose before beginning the scene. This table does not
introduce a timer, adversary, or forced loss by itself.

## 6. Full scene example

Your intended scene is “ask the ferryman about the missing keeper.” The scene
check rolls Hope 2, Fear 10: an obstacle. Keep the goal, but change the opening.

Because the scene already concerns the keeper, use Mara as the anchor without
an additional anchor roll. Choose Mystery. Fresh content dice roll Hope 6 and
Fear 6: **a conflicting witness / points toward a trusted ally**. These doubles
are a content pair; they do not trigger a second scene check or revelation.

Interpret the obstacle: the ferryman has given the watch a statement that names
Mara's friend as the last person seen with her. That friend is now being detained,
so you cannot question both witnesses freely. The statement is a claim, not
proof that the friend is guilty. You can speak with the ferryman, seek access to
the friend, or investigate another lead. No attack or resource loss happens merely
because the scene became more difficult.

At the scene's end, add the detained friend to an available pool slot only if
they became important. Record the ferryman's account as an account, not as a
confirmed description of what actually happened.

## 7. Make your own theme

A theme pack contains:

- A name and a sentence describing where it belongs.
- Twelve distinct subjects: people, places, objects, situations, or motifs.
- Twelve developments that describe changes, relationships, needs, or discoveries.
- An example interpretation rooted in the campaign.

Keep entries open enough to recombine. Avoid foregone conclusions such as
“the hero dies,” mandatory mechanical penalties, fixed answers to a mystery, or
instructions to ignore established facts. Three or four preferred motifs can
shape a pack without making every entry a variation of the same reveal.

Themes can come from the campaign setting: a salt-marsh frontier, a city built
around sleeping giants, a wandering theater troupe, or a region where memories
are traded. A setting-specific pack can replace a generic one; it need not add
another mandatory roll to every scene.

## Daggery interface notes

The Solo play workspace implements these tools. **Themes & pool** configures
the two theme columns and twelve anchor slots. **Draw inspiration** in Oracles
uses those saved settings, optionally pins a known anchor, and shows the content
dice and selected anchor. Saving an accepted interpretation records a journal
entry; it does not spend Fear, change resources, or complete a thread.

Keep theme content rolls separate from action and oracle outcomes. If a scene
check supplied the tone, retain that result when generating the content. Do not
recycle its faces as content indices or trigger another twist from content doubles.

## Design reference

[Word Mill Games' description of Mythic GME Second Edition](https://www.wordmillgames.com/page/mythic-gme.html)
explains its themed Elements Meaning Tables and recurring character/thread lists.
Those ideas inform this module's context-sensitive prompts and campaign pool.
The d12 tables, weighting template, tone handling, and examples above are original
Daggery Solo rules; this module does not reproduce Mythic's Fate Chart, table
entries, or Chaos Factor procedure.
