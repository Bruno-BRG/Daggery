# Duality GME: balance between Daggerheart and Ironsworn

Goal: Duality GME is "Ironsworn for Daggerheart". The character, the dice, the adversaries and the
level-up are Daggerheart. The way a solo story moves (vows, moves, momentum, oracles, progress) is
Ironsworn. This page checks where the two fit well, where Ironsworn took too much room, and what
we changed.

Rule of thumb used for every decision:

1. If Daggerheart already has a rule for it, use the Daggerheart rule. Ironsworn adds only what
   Daggerheart does not have (solo structure, tempo, oracles).
2. Do not build two resources for one job.
3. The character sheet is the truth for the hero. The trackers only add what the sheet cannot hold.

## 1. What fits well

| Area | How it fits |
| --- | --- |
| Dice | Strong hit = success with Hope or a critical. Weak hit = success with Fear. Miss = failure. One roll, three outcomes, no new dice. |
| Fear | Fear is the world's side of the table. It works as the chaos factor of a Mythic-style oracle and feeds the GM turn. This is the best fit in the system. |
| Foes | Tier and type give a rank (tier - 1 + role bonus). The Daggerheart stat block still gives the features. |
| Initiative | Ironsworn initiative is the Daggerheart spotlight: a strong hit keeps it, a weak hit or a miss passes it. |
| Health and spirit | They are the sheet's Hit Points and Stress. No second pool. |
| Vows, bonds | They become progress tracks. They also use countdown sizes (4, 6, 8) from Daggerheart. |
| Levels | Level-up is by milestone, as in Daggerheart. There is no experience count. |
| Conditions | One list on the sheet: Daggerheart (Hidden, Restrained, Vulnerable) and Ironsworn (eight debilities). |

## 2. Where the odds stand

We compared the outcome chances of the two dice systems (all combinations counted).

Ironsworn, action die d6 + stat, against two d10:

| Stat | Strong | Weak | Miss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| +1 | 15% | 40% | 45% |
| +2 | 23% | 44% | 33% |
| +3 | 33% | 44% | 23% |

Duality Dice, 2d12 + trait +2, against a Difficulty:

| Difficulty | Strong (Hope) | Weak (Fear) | Miss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 48% | 40% | 12% |
| 12 | 43% | 35% | 22% |
| 14 | 37% | 28% | 35% |
| 16 | 29% | 21% | 50% |
| 18 | 22% | 14% | 64% |

Findings:

- **Hit rate matches at Difficulty 14.** An Ironsworn +2 hero hits 67% of the time. A Duality +2
  trait at Difficulty 14 hits 65%. Use 14 as the "normal" Difficulty when you do not know what to
  choose. Use 12 for easy things, 16 for hard things, 18 for brutal ones.
- **Duality has more strong hits and fewer weak hits.** Hope is above Fear in half of all rolls, so
  strong hits are 37% against 23%. Weak hits are 28% against 44%. The result: more Momentum, more
  initiative, easier fights than in Ironsworn. This is a known tilt. It is a good tilt for a game
  about heroes, but watch it. If fights feel too easy, raise foe rank by one, or set Difficulty 16.
- **Fear grows fast.** About half of all rolls give Fear. The pool has a cap of 12. This is on
  purpose: the world pushes back as the story goes on. Burning Momentum is the way to hold it back.

## 3. Where Ironsworn took too much room (found in this review)

| # | Problem | Why it was wrong | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Stress had no Daggerheart rules. When Stress was full, Endure Stress took Momentum. | Daggerheart marks 1 Hit Point instead, and the last Stress makes you Vulnerable. The sheet already did this. The move contradicted the sheet. | Endure Stress now follows Daggerheart. Last Stress = Vulnerable. Stress you cannot mark = 1 Hit Point. |
| 2 | Last Hit Point went to Momentum, a Wounded table and then Face Death. | Daggerheart has death moves (Blaze of Glory, Avoid Death, Risk It All). They are a signature moment. Face Death replaced them. | Your last Hit Point is a death move. Choose Face Death or a Daggerheart one. Harm left over still costs Momentum. |
| 3 | Armor did nothing in fights. | Armor Slots are a core Daggerheart resource. | Endure Harm: mark an Armor Slot to reduce harm by 1. Make Camp can repair 2 slots. |
| 4 | Hero harm was fixed at 1 or 2. | Weapons and Proficiency are a core Daggerheart growth path. Levels did not change fight power. | Harm = 1 + half Proficiency (rounded up) with a real weapon. Foe ticks per harm stay the same, so a levelled hero wins fights faster. |
| 5 | Vulnerable and Shaken were separate, and Vulnerable (from full Stress) lived only in an alert. | Two ideas for the same state. | Vulnerable now shows on the conditions panel on its own when Stress is full. Shaken stays as the Ironsworn debility. |
| 6 | Burn Momentum removed Fear but gave nothing to the player. | A Daggerheart roll "with Hope" gives 1 Hope. A burned roll is a roll with Hope. | A burned roll with Fear counts as with Hope: +1 Hope. This ties the two resources together. |
| 7 | Resting had no effect on the world. | Daggerheart rests are downtime. Solo play needs a cost, or the player rests after every fight. | Make Camp: the world gains 1 Fear (short rest) or 1d4 Fear (long rest). This is a Duality GME rule. |
| 8 | Failure with Hope and failure with Fear paid the same price. | Daggerheart separates them: Hope failure is soft, Fear failure is hard. | Pay the Price: Hope failure is mild. Fear failure is severe. |
| 9 | Vows and bonds ignored the Daggerheart background and connections. | The character creation answers were never used. | Swear an Iron Vow: use them as first vows. |
| 10 | Experience counter in the tracker. | Ironsworn mechanic. Daggerheart levels by milestone. | Removed (earlier change). |
| 11 | Conditions lived in the Momentum panel. | The hero's state belongs on the sheet. | Moved to the sheet (earlier change). |

## 4. What is still Ironsworn-heavy (open points and advice)

These need a design decision from the table. We left them as they are, with our advice.

1. **Progress rolls use two d10.** This is the only roll that does not use the Duality Dice. We kept
   it on purpose. A d10 against a score of 0 to 10 is clean and well tested, and it keeps Hope and
   Fear out of long-term tracks. A Daggerheart version would roll the Duality Dice and add the
   score to compare with a Difficulty. If you want it, make it a table option, not the default.
2. **Supply is abstract.** Daggerheart has consumables and gold (handfuls, bags, chests). A good
   bridge: 1 Supply = one use of a consumable or one handful of gold. We do not enforce it. If you
   track consumables on the sheet, let them back your Supply.
3. **Harm is a number, not a threshold.** Daggerheart weapons roll damage against Major and Severe
   thresholds. Here a foe deals harm by rank. This is the biggest simplification. It keeps the
   fight fast and the numbers small. Armor and Proficiency now touch it (rows 3 and 4 above).
4. **The foe track replaces adversary Hit Points.** Foe Stress, thresholds and the Difficulty on
   the stat block are not used. Advice: use the adversary's own Difficulty as the roll target when
   you have a stat block. Use rank only when you invented the foe.
5. **Momentum and Hope overlap.** Both are "how well it is going". We keep them apart by job:
   Hope is spent on Experiences and class features. Momentum is spent only by burning it. Burning
   gives Hope (row 6 above). If the table finds both heavy, drop Momentum gains on strong hits to +1 only.
6. **Journey and scene tracks are Ironsworn shapes.** They fit Daggerheart's countdowns well. Keep
   using the countdown sizes (4, 6, 8) for scenes.
7. **Level and rank do not scale together.** A tier 4 hero still faces a Minor foe in 3 boxes. The
   rank of foes scales with adversary tier, so it is fine for fights. For vows, choose the rank by
   the story, not by your level.

## 5. How to play it well (short advice)

- Keep Difficulty at 14 by default. Raise it when the scene is unfair, lower it when the hero has
  the advantage.
- Spend Hope on Experiences before the roll. Burn Momentum after the roll. This keeps the
  Daggerheart decision and the Ironsworn decision at different moments.
- Treat Fear as the world's turn. When Fear is high, make the foe act. When Fear is low, let the
  hero lead.
- Rest costs Fear. Rest when the story wants it, not by habit.
- Level up at milestones: a Grave or greater vow, a finished arc, or when the table agrees.
