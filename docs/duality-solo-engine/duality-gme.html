Duality GME

An independent solo-play supplement for Daggerheart. Version 0.1.

How to Play

  1. Create your character with the Daggerheart rules.
  2. Take an Oath on an inciting incident (roll a Plot Hook if you need one).
  3. Begin a Session every time you sit down to play.
  4. Envision, then make moves. When you do something a move describes, make it: usually an action roll with the trait that fits.
  5. Read the outcome as a strong hit, weak hit or miss. On a miss, Pay the Price.
  6. Mark progress on oaths, expeditions, connections and scene challenges; roll progress to finish them.
  7. When you stop, End a Session.

Spend Hope as Daggerheart allows. Use the tables as your oracle. Play with paper and dice or any tools you prefer; no app is required.

Action Roll Outcomes

Make the Daggerheart action roll (Duality Dice + trait against a Difficulty), then read it as a move outcome:

Daggerheart resultMove outcome
Critical successStrong hit, with the critical's own benefits
Success with HopeStrong hit
Success with FearWeak hit: you get it, at a cost or with less
Failure with HopeMiss: Pay the Price
Failure with FearMiss: Pay the Price; the GM may spend 1 Fear to make it severe

Rolls with Fear still give the GM Fear, and rolls with Hope still give you Hope.

Difficulty by rank: Minor 10, Serious 13, Grave 15, Dire 18, Legendary 20.

Progress Tracks

Oaths, expeditions, connections and scene challenges each have a progress track of 10 boxes, and each box holds 4 ticks. The rank sets how much one “mark progress” fills:

Progress roll: roll two d10 (the challenge dice) and compare each to your progress score (full boxes, 0 to 10). Beat both: a strong hit. Beat one: a weak hit. Beat neither: a miss. Ties go to the challenge dice. Matching challenge dice: a twist or an extreme result. Progress rolls never generate Hope or Fear.

Ask the Oracle

Pick a likelihood and roll the Duality Dice; a yes needs the total shown. If the dice match (doubles), the answer comes with an extreme result or a twist. For open questions, roll Meaning.

Pay the Price

On a miss, choose the most obvious negative outcome, or roll on the Pay the Price table. A 12 means two prices. A severe price (failure with Fear, the GM spends 1 Fear) rolls twice and both happen.

Begin a Session

  1. Set or review your flags: content you want to avoid or handle with care.
  2. Recap where you left off.
  3. Set the scene: where your character is and what they intend.
  4. Optional: spotlight a danger, opportunity or insight elsewhere: roll a Vignette, envision it, then gain a Hope as you return to your character.

Inciting Incident

Roll Objective + Opposition, then Reward + Twist; keep the Twist hidden.

Session Moves

Quest Moves

Adventure Moves

Exploration

Undertake an Expedition across dangerous ground: give it a rank and a progress track, and roll for each segment. At a waypoint, Explore a Waypoint: a strong hit finds an opportunity or, on a critical, a Discovery; a weak hit gives a detail and a choice; a miss is a peril: Pay the Price. On a failure with Fear, make a Fear Check: a pass spends 1 Fear and you Confront Chaos. Finish an Expedition with a progress roll.

Connections

Make a Connection (Presence): strong hit, they become a connection with a role and a rank; weak hit, they ask something first; miss, Pay the Price. Develop Your Relationship to mark progress. Forge a Bond with a progress roll: once per session a bond can help you (an advantage die, d6).

Scene Challenge

For a tense scene against a threat or a clock (a chase, an infiltration, a debate): name the objective and give it a grave progress track, then a tension clock of 4 segments at a disadvantage, 6 on even footing, 8 with an advantage. Finish the Scene with a progress roll when the track or the clock is full, or the story ends it.

Rumors and Leads

When the next scene needs a spark, roll a rumor: it points at a person, a place or an oath.

Combat

Join the Fray with Instinct (or Agility): on a success with Hope you act first; with Fear, or a failure, the GM takes the spotlight first. Fights use Daggerheart's stat blocks and the Duality combat tables for the GM turn. For a minor fight, Battle instead: one action roll.

When the spotlight passes to the GM, pick the adversary that makes the most sense (or that has acted least) and make a Fear Check. Fails: it acts for free; roll Tactics. Passes: roll Fear Spend, pay the cost. Chain after each GM action; the GM turn ends on the first failed check.

Adversaries

A Fear feature costs what its stat block says; the other results cost 1 Fear. If the engine cannot pay, it rolls Tactics instead. A Hard Move result here is a severe Pay the Price.

Fate Moves

Rest and Make Camp

Make Camp in the wild: take a Daggerheart rest (the GM gains Fear as the rules say), then roll Rest and Camp for what happens. A Random Event result: roll a Vignette.

Names

Hope die: given name; Fear die: column; then a family name.

NPCs

Role and Motive: who they are and what they want.

Meaning (Open Questions)

For what a yes/no cannot answer. Roll both Duality Dice per word: the Hope die picks the row, the Fear die picks the column (odd = A, even = B). Action + Subject for events, Descriptor + Subject for things and places.

Set the Difficulty

Duality GME · A consistent ruling before the dice.

When an action calls for a roll, set the Difficulty before rolling. Use facts already established in the scene, not the number you hope to beat.

1. Use an existing value first

  1. If the rule or move specifies a Difficulty, use it.
  2. If you are overcoming an adversary or NPC with a defined Difficulty, use theirs.
  3. If the move tells you to use a challenge's rank, use that rank's Difficulty.

Do not add obstacle increases to these values. Only use the procedure below when none of these sources provides the Difficulty. A nearby progress track does not automatically set every action's Difficulty; the move must call for its rank.

2. Otherwise, start at 13

13 is the default for an uncertain action. You do not need to justify why it is not harder. Check for three distinct obstacles:

ObstacleWhat must already be true?
ResistanceA relevant safeguard or committed opposition stands in your way: a reinforced lock, guards on alert, or someone under explicit orders to refuse.
DemandThe result requires unusual precision, reach or personal concession: a tiny mechanism, an exceptional gap, or a request that risks someone's position.
ConditionsSomething concretely hinders the attempt: darkness without a light, unstable footing, or a crowd preventing conversation.
Distinct obstaclesDifficulty
None13
One15
Two18
Three20

Count each category at most once. Count the same fact only once, even if it could fit two categories. Ordinary uncertainty is already part of 13: a normal lock is not automatically a Resistance obstacle. Do not invent obstacles to reach a preferred number. If no established fact supports an obstacle, do not count it.

3. Easy, certain or impossible?

  • Difficulty 10: no obstacles, and a concrete fact makes the task especially simple, but uncertainty remains.
  • No roll: success is evident, or failure would change nothing.
  • Impossible right now: do not roll; first change the situation.

Judge the task, not the hero. Traits and Experiences already modify the roll; do not count them again when setting Difficulty. Do not also use the same fact as an obstacle increase and as disadvantage, or as both the reason for Difficulty 10 and an advantage die. Separate facts may affect the roll separately.

Example: convincing a guard

If the guard has a defined Difficulty, use it. Otherwise:

  • Asking a willing person for ordinary information may need no roll.
  • Convincing an undecided person to help with a reasonable task: 13.
  • Convincing a guard explicitly ordered to deny entry: Resistance, 15.
  • Asking that guard to abandon their post and risk their job: Resistance + Demand, 18.
  • Making that request amid a crowd that prevents conversation: all three, 20.

Lock it before rolling

Say the number and the reason: "Difficulty 15: the guard has orders to refuse." Do not change it after seeing the dice. Reassess only when the situation changes.

Danger is not Difficulty. A simple crossing can still have a deadly fall. Danger changes the consequence of failure; it does not automatically raise the number. This is a Duality GME procedure for consistent rulings, not a guarantee that every action has the same chance of success.

Hazards and Traps

Make the reaction roll named. Difficulty and damage by tier.

NPCs and Places

Loot and Treasure

Roll Loot and a Detail that makes it a story.

Wilds

For each new region or day of travel: the land, the weather, and a landmark to steer by.

Sites

For dungeons, ruins, and lairs. For each new area roll Area, Exits, and Contents. The first time you roll Area 12, you have found the heart of the site.

Settlements

Name it from First + Second (Ash + ford: Ashford), then roll Size, Known for, and Trouble.

Journeys

For each leg of travel make a Fear Check. Pass: spend 1 Fear and roll Danger. Fail: roll Discovery.
