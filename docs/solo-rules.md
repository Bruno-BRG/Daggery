# Daggery Solo: Hope & Fear Oracles

Version 0.2 — playable homebrew draft, not an official Daggerheart supplement.
Supports one hero alone, one hero with a lightweight companion, and a small party
controlled by one player. The oracle probabilities have been checked exhaustively.
Combat pacing and companion balance still need playtesting.

## Start playing

You are the player when deciding what your hero does and the referee when
interpreting the world's response. Record facts as you discover them; do not
prepare the ending.

1. Create your character or characters using Daggerheart. Choose a personal
   objective, someone who matters, and something that is already going wrong.
2. Write one unresolved **thread**: a question, conflict, or obligation. Give its
   opposition a motive and a next step.
3. Frame a scene: where you are, what you want here, and what could go wrong.
4. If a world fact is uncertain, ask the **question oracle**. If your character
   attempts something risky, make a normal **action roll** instead.
5. Resolve the result. Use a **world turn** when the fiction or an action outcome
   calls for the GM to respond. Use a meaning prompt only if you need inspiration.
6. End the scene when its immediate question has been answered. Record a fact,
   a changed relationship, and your next intention. Skip entries that did not change.

**Track:** the usual character resources, the normal GM Fear pool, and your
threads. Companion mode also uses the small companion profile in section 8.
Add at most one progress/consequence countdown pair for a complex scene.
There is no additional chaos, momentum, or oracle currency.

## What comes from Daggerheart

Keep character action and reaction rolls, printed abilities, damage, adversary
attacks, rests, and advancement under the normal rules. Action rolls still use
their usual Difficulty and modifiers; matching Duality Dice remain critical.
Preserve the usual action-roll Hope/Fear rewards and the different reaction-roll
behavior. Fear starts at one per PC, caps at twelve, and persists between sessions.
Adversaries keep their d20 attack rolls; an oracle does not replace them.
Use the published countdown procedures when a stat block calls for one.

These reference points come from the [official Daggerheart SRD 2.0](https://www.daggerheart.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/DH_SRD_2_2026_08_25.pdf),
printed pages 47–49, 52, 86–91, and 94. Everything described below as an oracle,
selection procedure, or solo pacing rule is this draft's homebrew layer.

## 1. The question oracle

Use this to discover an unknown **fact**, not to decide whether your character's
action succeeds.

Examples: “Is the tower occupied?”, “Does the captain recognize this seal?”, or
“Is there another way into the cellar?”

1. Write a concrete yes/no question.
2. Choose its likelihood from established facts, before rolling. Default to Even.
3. Roll your Hope d12 and Fear d12 without modifiers.
4. Matching dice answer **Yes — revelation**, regardless of the total.
5. Otherwise, a sum meeting the target answers **Yes**; a lower sum answers **No**.
6. The higher die tells you the answer's tone. Establish one concrete fact and act.

| Likelihood of “yes” | Target | Actual yes chance, including doubles |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Very likely | 8 | 87.5% |
| Likely | 11 | 72.2% |
| Even | 14 | 50.0% |
| Unlikely | 17 | 30.6% |
| Very unlikely | 20 | 16.7% |

Certain or impossible facts need no roll. A locked-in fact cannot be reversed
by rephrasing it or asking the opposite question. Revisit it only after an
in-world change or new evidence.

| Result | Establish this |
| --- | --- |
| Yes with Hope | The fact is true, with a useful opening or helpful detail. |
| Yes with Fear | The fact is true, with a troubling condition or hidden risk. |
| No with Hope | The fact is false, but there is an alternative or useful lead. |
| No with Fear | The fact is false, and the reason makes your situation harder. |
| Matching dice | The fact is true, with a surprising connection and a usable opening. |

**Yes does not mean good.** If you ask “Is the bridge damaged?” and roll Yes with
Hope, the damage is real, but you might find a sound maintenance cable. Yes with
Fear might reveal damage hidden beneath the walkway. Neither result automatically
makes your character fall.

**Oracle rolls never grant or spend Hope or Fear, clear Stress, activate character
features, tick countdowns, or hand the opposition a turn.** Their tone adds context;
it is not a free attack or an additional mechanical penalty. The established fact
can make a later action risky. Asking more questions does not advance time unless
your character actually spends time investigating.

Do not use traits, Experiences, advantage, or reroll features on an oracle. Those
belong to your character's action rolls. A hero who searches for a secret door
rolls an action if that search is risky; do not roll an oracle afterward to cancel
a successful search. A success discovers the useful thing that was at stake.

### Why the neutral target is 14

There are 144 equally likely ordered d12 pairs. Of those, 12 are doubles. At a
target of 14, 60 non-matching pairs meet the target; the 12 automatic affirmative
doubles bring the total to 72 out of 144. Hope and Fear are equally represented
among the non-matching affirmative results.

This target is for the oracle only. It does not change the Difficulty of normal
character actions. The likelihood bands are not exact complements because every
matching pair answers yes; the displayed percentages include that effect.

## 2. Starting a scene

Write your expected scene in one sentence: “I reach the lighthouse to ask its
keeper about the missing ship.” If the approach and situation are already clear,
start there without a roll.

If arriving at an uncertain destination, or returning after meaningful time has
passed, make one **scene check** with the two d12s:

| Dice | Scene opening |
| --- | --- |
| Matching dice | An unexpected connection changes the premise. Roll a twist prompt. |
| Hope exceeds Fear by 6 or more | Keep your goal; introduce an opportunity. |
| Fear exceeds Hope by 6 or more | Keep your goal; introduce an obstacle or changed circumstance. |
| Anything else | Begin as expected. |

Test for matching dice first. Do not compare the total to a Difficulty. This is
not a question oracle or an action roll and has no resource effects.

Scene checks leave the opening unchanged 62.5% of the time. Opportunities and
obstacles each occur about 14.6% of the time; twists occur about 8.3%.
Do not make another scene check just because the opening is inconvenient.

An obstacle changes how you can pursue the scene's goal; it does not erase the
adventure. The keeper might have vanished, leaving a wet trail and an urgent note.
An opportunity might put a willing witness at the door. Use one change, not all of them.

## 3. Meaning prompts

For genre-specific scenes and recurring campaign elements, use the companion
module [Thematic scene pools](solo-themes.md). It adds six original d12 theme
packs, a weighted pool of existing threads/NPCs/places, and scene-purpose prompts.
The general table below remains a fallback; do not roll both unless a second
unanswered detail actually needs it.

When you know that something happens but not what, roll two d12s. Read Hope as
an action and Fear as a subject. Combine them into one event that fits a known
thread, NPC motive, or location. These are inspiration rolls without resource
or countdown effects.

| d12 | Action — Hope die | Subject — Fear die |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Conceal | A promise |
| 2 | Protect | A passage |
| 3 | Demand | A stranger |
| 4 | Break | A resource |
| 5 | Follow | A memory |
| 6 | Exchange | A faction |
| 7 | Awaken | A refuge |
| 8 | Divide | A secret |
| 9 | Restore | A danger |
| 10 | Reveal | A relationship |
| 11 | Abandon | An authority |
| 12 | Transform | A destination |

“Protect a secret” could mean that the frightened keeper is hiding someone, not
that every mystery in the campaign gains a new subplot. Take the first plausible
interpretation. If none fits, use only one word or choose the nearest useful entry.

### Twist prompts — d12

| d12 | Change |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Two apparently separate threads share a cause. |
| 2 | A familiar person has an unexpected responsibility. |
| 3 | A supposed obstacle is holding back something worse. |
| 4 | An old promise has become urgent. |
| 5 | Your destination is moving or changing. |
| 6 | Someone else is pursuing the same objective. |
| 7 | The apparent prize is a means to another end. |
| 8 | Help comes from someone with a conflicting motive. |
| 9 | A past decision has an unforeseen consequence. |
| 10 | A local dispute matters to a larger faction. |
| 11 | A missing person or object returns in a changed role. |
| 12 | The threat and an ally need the same thing. |

Surprise may reinterpret an assumption. It cannot retroactively erase a confirmed
success, undo a paid ability, or contradict an established fact without an in-world
explanation. Introduce evidence the hero can interact with.

## 4. Action outcomes and world turns

Before an action roll, name the intended effect, Difficulty, and a plausible risk.
Resolve the normal game mechanics first. A failure and a Fear result on the same
roll create **one response**, not two separate punishments.

| Action outcome | Solo interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Critical | Accomplish the intent and identify an additional opening. |
| Success with Hope | Accomplish the intent. Describe the world's response and keep acting. |
| Success with Fear | Accomplish the intent. Take one world turn; its cost cannot negate that success. |
| Failure with Hope | The intent is not achieved as stated. Take one world turn with a recoverable setback. |
| Failure with Fear | The intent is not achieved as stated. Take one world turn with a decisive setback. |

A **world turn** is this draft's procedure for choosing the GM response:

1. Resolve triggered printed reactions at their actual timing. Do not defer them
   until after the result if their text says otherwise.
2. Start with an established threat's motive or the risk you named before rolling.
   If its response is clear, use it. Otherwise roll on the response prompts below.
3. Choose one concrete move with severity appropriate to the result and situation.
   Before resolving it, check the Fear policy: a legal signature action may replace
   this ordinary move. Pay its full cost before activating it.
4. Apply the chosen move's actual rules, if any. An attack still rolls to hit; a hazard uses its
   listed resolution. A frightening description is not automatically HP damage.
5. If no signature was activated this beat, check whether the Fear policy adds a
   paid setup move after the ordinary move. Then return to the hero's decision.

Use a world turn as well when your hero knowingly ignores an imminent danger or
takes an action with an unavoidable consequence. Oracle Fear faces alone do not
trigger this procedure.

### Response prompts — d12

| d12 | What does the opposition change? |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | An NPC acts on an established motive. |
| 2 | An opponent changes position or blocks an approach. |
| 3 | Evidence reveals an unwelcome truth. |
| 4 | A route or opportunity begins to close. |
| 5 | A named deadline draws closer. |
| 6 | Equipment, supplies, or composure come under pressure. |
| 7 | A relationship or obligation complicates the choice. |
| 8 | Someone or something important is threatened. |
| 9 | The environment presents a concrete hazard. |
| 10 | A present adversary uses a relevant action or attacks. |
| 11 | Someone offers a useful but costly bargain. |
| 12 | An existing off-screen threat takes its next step. |

The table chooses a direction, not extra damage or an invented mandatory cost.
For a recoverable setback, telegraph danger, worsen position, or impose the
predeclared minor cost. For a decisive setback, make a foreshadowed danger arrive,
lose an opportunity, or resolve the named serious risk. Keep the response relevant.

If a countdown is already representing the named risk, its advance is the response
for that risk. Do not also add a second penalty describing the same thing.

## 5. Fear without a GM

Fear remains the opposition's resource. Update it for normal game triggers,
including rests. It does not rise because you consult an oracle, change scenes,
or run out of ideas.

**Automatic spending policy — homebrew:**

- Before an encounter, nominate one signature Fear feature for each important
  adversary or environment, and record its printed cost and trigger.
- When its trigger occurs, use that nominated feature if the pool has at least
  **3 Fear**, can pay the full cost, and its targets and conditions are legal.
  A reaction fires at its printed timing. An action feature chosen during the
  world turn takes the place of the ordinary move, not an additional free move.
- Make at most one such automatic activation during a **beat**: from a character
  action's declaration through its resulting world turn. If a reaction used it,
  do not also activate a signature action in that beat. Mandatory non-Fear reactions
  still follow their printed text.
- If a world turn has no eligible signature activation and none was used this beat,
  then, at **3+ Fear**, spend **1 Fear** after the ordinary move to make one additional
  **setup move**: warn of approaching trouble, reposition an opponent, or reveal an
  impending threat. It causes no immediate damage, marks no resources, and advances
  no countdown. The hero gets a chance to respond to this new setup.
- Below that threshold, use the ordinary world response without spending Fear.
  Do not pay Fear merely because an action failed or rolled with Fear.

When a feature needs another paid activation to become legal, include that cost
too. This policy never grants a free additional spotlight. It does not enable
an out-of-turn interrupt unless the feature's trigger already permits it.

If multiple signatures qualify, use the one belonging to the threat most directly
involved in the action. Break a genuine tie with a die. Name the priorities before
rolling rather than choosing whichever result hurts most afterward.

This policy is a starting pace, not a balance guarantee. For a gentler campaign,
change the threshold to 5 before the session. Features requiring more Fear wait
until affordable. The normal Fear cap still applies; excess Fear is lost rather
than converted into another penalty. Fear does not require a separate chaos track.

## 6. NPCs and opposition

Give an important NPC four entries: **name, want, leverage, limit**. Their motive
answers most questions about their behavior. Consult the oracle only when two
plausible interpretations remain.

For a new NPC motive, roll d12:

| d12 | Want |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Keep someone safe |
| 2 | Recover something lost |
| 3 | Avoid exposure |
| 4 | Fulfill an obligation |
| 5 | Escape someone's control |
| 6 | Secure a scarce resource |
| 7 | Win recognition |
| 8 | Change the local order |
| 9 | Preserve a tradition |
| 10 | Settle a grievance |
| 11 | Discover forbidden knowledge |
| 12 | Leave a dangerous place |

For combat, write an adversary's immediate plan before rolling: a main attack or
action, a setup tactic, its objective, and when it would withdraw or bargain.
When an adversary takes the spotlight and the next move is unclear, roll d12:

| d12 | Intent |
| ---: | --- |
| 1–6 | Use its main attack/action to pursue its motive. |
| 7–9 | Use its setup tactic or reposition toward its objective. |
| 10–11 | Pursue the objective directly: escape, seize, break, protect. |
| 12 | Defend, regroup, bargain, or withdraw if that fits its current condition. |

An intent grants no new ability, free damage, or extra movement. If it is illegal
or nonsensical, use the main plan instead. If no attack can reach a target, it
must approach or do something else legal. An injured enemy can surrender before
all its HP are marked. Printed group attacks and reactions still apply.

The default automation chooses one ordinary spotlight per world turn. Additional
spotlights, including repeat activations enabled by Relentless, require normal
Fear costs and an explicit decision before resolving them. Multi-spotlight boss
automation is outside this first draft; do not silently ignore a boss's features
and assume the resulting encounter represents its normal strength.

## 7. Exploration and longer challenges

For a complex goal, write a **progress countdown** and, if needed, a linked
**consequence countdown**. Give each a concrete completion event and a trigger.
Example: find the smugglers' exit before the chamber floods.

Use the existing Daggerheart dynamic-countdown advancement rules rather than
adding a second progress system. For an original solo scene, begin with a progress
countdown of **4** for a short objective or **6** for a longer one. A consequence
countdown of **6** is a starting point when there is an active threat. These starting
values are playtest recommendations, not replacements for values in stat blocks.

- Only relevant actions advance a dynamic scene countdown. A roll to read a
  decorative inscription does not advance finding the exit unless it helps.
- Printed standard countdowns retain their own action-roll trigger. Do not
  replace them with the dynamic procedure.
- An oracle, scene check, or prompt roll advances neither type.
- If both linked countdowns reach zero on the same action, resolve both:
  the goal is achieved as its stated consequence arrives.
- A risk already resolved by a countdown is not charged again as a world move.
  Independent, pre-established reactions may still occur.
- Never create an abstract progress countdown for damage already tracked by HP.

Discoveries should change the next decision: a usable route, an NPC's motive,
evidence, a resource, or a danger you can prepare for. A necessary clue is not
withheld behind repeated rolls; failures can make reaching it costly or dangerous.

## 8. Three ways to play

Choose a mode at the start of a session. All three use the same oracle, scene
checks, world-turn procedure, and Fear policy.

| Mode | Characters you manage | PCs counted for Fear and rest rules | Starting approach |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Lone hero | One normal PC | 1 | Small conflicts, escape routes, and personal stakes. |
| Hero + companion | One normal PC and one support profile | 1 | The same starting encounters, with limited assistance. |
| Small party | Two or three normal PCs | Actual PC count | Published encounter guidance for that party size. |

Companions are not counted as full PCs for encounter budgets. Their support is
not equivalent to another character's independent attacks and class features.
If you add or remove PCs between sessions, keep the existing Fear pool; its
initial one-per-PC amount is a campaign-start rule, not a session reset.

### A. One hero alone

Fewer allies means fewer tactical options and less room to absorb bad outcomes.
For the first lone-hero playtest:

- Start with one same-tier Standard adversary or a noncombat obstacle. This is
  a conservative test encounter, not a promise of safety.
- Set an objective other than eliminating every enemy: reach the door, rescue
  the prisoner, interrupt a signal. Establish a way to retreat when the fiction allows.
- Use known attack damage, Armor, Stress, and HP rules. Do not compensate by
  secretly changing die results, doubling Hope gains, or removing Fear features.
- Add opponents gradually after observing the hero's actual defenses and tools.
  An adversary with the **Solo** role is designed as an enemy role; its name does
  not mean it is calibrated for one player character.

### B. One hero with a lightweight companion

Create a companion with a **name, motive, bond, and specialty**. A specialty is
one focused area such as scouting, medicine, diplomacy, ruins, or close protection.
Their tier follows the hero's tier. This is an original support profile, not a
second character class or a replacement for a Ranger's printed companion rules.

| Statistic | Companion value |
| --- | --- |
| Hit Points | 3 |
| Stress | 3 |
| Evasion | 10 + hero's tier |
| Major / Severe thresholds | (6 + 3 × tier) / (12 + 3 × tier) |
| Armor Slots | 0 |
| Hope, loadout, Experiences | None |

Track marked HP and Stress. At tier 1 the companion has Evasion 11 and thresholds
9/15. Incoming attacks roll against their Evasion; damage marks HP by the usual
threshold procedure. Do not use their Stress as a substitute HP pool.

**One contribution per beat.** When present, capable, and fictionally positioned
to help, the companion can use one of the following. Mark its cost immediately.
They cannot begin a contribution while all Stress is marked or while incapacitated.
Paying their last available Stress still resolves that contribution, then leaves
them Vulnerable and unable to contribute again until Stress is cleared.

| Contribution | Cost | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assist | Hero spends 1 Hope; companion marks 1 Stress | Before the hero rolls an action within the companion's specialty, add the normal advantage d6. Follow the usual advantage/disadvantage interaction; this creates no second stacking advantage die. |
| Strike together | Companion marks 1 Stress | After the hero's successful attack, if the companion can reach or threaten the same target, add 1d6 to that attack's damage. Roll it after calculating any critical damage; this bonus gets no additional critical maximum. Apply the combined total once against thresholds. |
| Cover | Companion marks 1 Stress | When a hero within Very Close range would mark HP from damage the companion can physically intercept, reduce the HP marked by 1, minimum 0. Decide after normal Armor use. This cannot prevent direct damage, affect multiple targets, or redirect the attack to the companion as well. |

This contribution limit spans the hero's action and the world turn it triggers.
If Assist was used, Cover and Strike together are unavailable until the next beat.
A new oracle question does not start another beat or refresh assistance. A separate
unavoidable hazard gets its own beat when actually resolved.

The companion has no independent combat spotlight or free attack. They can act
naturally in the story, but a contribution only exists when they can actually help.
If a risky operation involves both characters, resolve the hero's action and use
Assist when appropriate. Play a companion's independent dangerous adventures with
a full second PC if you want to resolve them in detail; off-screen narration does
not grant free objective completion or bypass the hero's action rolls.

**Being threatened.** The companion can be targeted when the fiction and an
opponent's motive support it. Follow any target-selection text in a stat block.
For an otherwise genuine tie between equally exposed hero and companion, use
an unmodified d12: 1–6 hero, 7–12 companion. Do not use oracle likelihood to choose
who is hit. Area effects can affect both; sharing a beat does not grant immunity.

When all Stress is marked, the companion is Vulnerable and cannot contribute
until Stress is cleared. If an effect requires further Stress, mark HP instead.
At all HP marked, they are incapacitated and cannot act or contribute. For this
support mode they do not make a PC death move: leave them injured, captured, or
needing rescue as the situation establishes. Lethal stakes for companions must
be agreed before the scene; do not introduce death as a surprise bookkeeping rule.

**Recovery.** After an uninterrupted hero short rest, clear 1 companion HP and
1 companion Stress; after an uninterrupted hero long rest, clear all of both.
An interrupted rest gives only the recovery corresponding to the rest benefits
the hero actually receives. The companion grants no extra downtime moves and adds
no extra Fear to rests. Printed healing may help them if its target wording allows
an allied creature; effects requiring a PC do not gain a new eligible target.

They count as an ally where ordinary targeting permits an NPC ally. They are not
eligible for effects requiring a second PC, and cannot initiate Tag Team rolls.
Their contributions do not generate Hope/Fear or count as additional action rolls
for countdowns. Assist and Strike together can enhance a qualifying hero action;
that action still grants its own normal resources only once.

Use one support companion per hero in this draft. Do not apply this profile on top
of a Ranger companion, summoned creature, or another printed feature. To promote
them into a full PC, switch to small-party mode between scenes and remove this
profile's contributions and recovery rules; do not retain both sets of benefits.

### C. One player controlling a small party

Create two or three normal PCs with distinct motives and useful connections.
Track their resources and spotlight separately. Choose which PC leads a risky
action before rolling; another PC can help only through a legal party mechanic.
Do not roll every party member until one succeeds at the same unchanged task.

For this solo mode, treat each PC as a separate player for party-feature
participation and per-player session limits. This is an explicit house convention:
for example, each PC may initiate its own once-per-session Tag Team under the
normal Hope cost. This grants no extra uses to an individual character and does
not change feature costs or outcome rules.

Maintain one shared Fear pool, not one per character. Apply resource changes
according to the actual roll type. Helping and damage dice are not extra action
rolls. A group or Tag Team action is one dramatic beat for this draft's automatic
Fear policy, while its printed resource and countdown rules still apply.

Use published encounter guidance for the actual PC count and capabilities;
start at the easier end while learning to manage multiple sheets. PCs can
disagree in the fiction, but establish their motives instead of asking the oracle
to override your decisions about characters you control. The oracle supplies
unknown world facts and uncertain NPC reactions, not a command to choose a PC's
action or spend that PC's resources.

### Defeat and campaign tone

Full PCs use normal death moves by default. If you prefer a campaign without protagonist
death, agree before play to a separate defeat rule: at the point a death move
would occur, end the conflict in capture, rescue, or another lasting setback,
then resume after a fictionally justified long rest. Do not collect both sets of
benefits. This is an optional change to the game's stakes, not an oracle effect.

## 9. Session rhythm

At the beginning, recap your last decision, pick one active thread, and write the
next scene's goal. Resume your recorded resources rather than resetting them.

After a completed scene, update only what changed. When you actually rest, use
the normal rest rules and associated Fear gain. At a long rest, advance one
relevant long-term threat according to its recorded rest trigger; do not roll
additional attacks just because your journal entry ends.

At the session's end, record the next scene's opening, your resources, and one
unanswered question. Apply normal character advancement at an appropriate
narrative milestone; oracle questions and journal word counts do not earn XP.

## 10. Example: the dark lighthouse

You want to find why ships are vanishing. Your current thread is “Who is using
the lighthouse after its keeper disappeared?” An established threat is a salvage
crew hiding below it. Your scene goal is to reach the lantern room.

**Scene check:** Hope 8, Fear 5. No doubles and a gap of 3: the scene begins as
expected. You reach the tower. No Hope or Fear changes.

**Question:** “Is there a usable side entrance?” Likely, target 11. Hope 9, Fear 4:
13, Yes with Hope. A loose service door opens onto a sheltered staircase. You
record it. This does not award Hope or spend a character action.

**Action:** You climb past a listening sentry. You declare the risk: being heard
will force you into a confrontation. Difficulty 12, trait +2. Hope 5, Fear 8 gives
15 with Fear. You reach the upper landing; apply the normal Fear gain once. The
world turn introduces the cost: the sentry heard the door below and begins
following your route. Your climb is not undone.

Suppose the Fear pool is now 3 and there is no eligible signature feature. The
automatic policy spends 1, leaving 2, and adds a setup: the sentry signals a
second crew member to watch the exit. No immediate damage or extra countdown
ticks are added. You can now hide, bargain, hurry onward, or confront the sentry.

**Meaning:** You want a clue to the crew's purpose. Hope 6 and Fear 8 gives
“Exchange a secret.” Their ledger records payments for keeping a particular
shipping route dark. Finding the ledger should arise from your next fictional
action; the prompt itself does not teleport it into your hand.

## Playtest checklist

Play three short scenes: discovery, negotiation, and a small conflict. Record:

- Did the oracle answer the question without requiring several follow-up rolls?
- Did Hope/Fear tone create a meaningful distinction without negating success?
- Did the automated Fear procedure produce pressure without repeated attacks?
- Did progress and consequences resolve once each, without duplicate penalties?
- Did you spend more time deciding and imagining than consulting tables?
- For one hero, how many hostile spotlights occurred before retreat or defeat?
- With a companion, did the three contributions compete for use, and did recovery
  make the companion available without eliminating meaningful risk?
- With a small party, did spotlight and resource tracking remain manageable?

Tune one thing at a time after the session: encounter size, countdown length,
or Fear activation threshold. Keep the verified oracle math fixed unless you
deliberately change its intended odds.

## Daggery integration notes

Choose **Daggerheart Solo** in campaign creation or settings. The Solo play
workspace contains oracles, scene preparation, thematic pools, a journal, a
companion tracker when enabled, and this rules reference. All three party modes
are available. See [campaign systems and implementation](game-systems.md).

Oracle, scene, and prompt results use a dedicated server API rather than the
character sheet's action handler. Questions, likelihood targets, both Duality
faces, answers, interpretations, and the rules version are persisted per campaign.
The player accepts interpretations into the journal; a random prompt does not
automatically change character resources or established facts.

Companion contributions and countdown/Fear procedures use an explicit action
beat, so consulting an oracle does not refresh them. Companion Assist pays its
costs together and the selected character's sheet consumes the prepared advantage
once. Signature triggers and full Fear costs are shown for confirmation. Damage,
Cover's HP reduction, and fictional consequences remain player-resolved.

Campaign Fear is the same pool used by the character sheets and game table.
Solo preparations and history are private, campaign-scoped, and unavailable in
group mode. Switching back to a solo ruleset retains them.

Run `node --import tsx tools/check_solo_rules.ts` to verify every one of the
144 face pairs for all five likelihoods and the scene check.
